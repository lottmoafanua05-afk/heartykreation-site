When building your own website makes sense

We sell websites, and we are about to tell you when not to buy one. That is not a trick. It is just that the wrong customer costs everyone more than the sale is worth.

Do it yourself if three things are true

You have a free weekend. Not two free hours across three weeks. A real block of time where you can start a thing and finish it. Building your own site in twenty minute pieces is how a project dies at step four.

You are willing to be the one who fixes it. When a form stops sending or a renewal notice arrives, that is you. Not hard, but it never fully goes away.

Your business is simple to explain. One or two services, a phone number, some proof, and a location. The more moving parts, the more the do it yourself route turns into a second job.

What the work actually is

Nine steps, in order: decide the one thing a visitor should do, pick a name and buy the domain, choose where the site lives, build the five sections every small business page needs, write the words, handle photos, set up your Google Business Profile, connect the domain, and run a go live checklist.

None of it is hard on its own. The whole list in one sitting is what makes it a weekend.

The two places people quit

Buying the domain. Not the buying part, the extras. Registrars push add ons at checkout, and people either pay for things they do not need or buy the domain inside a website builder, which makes it painful to move later. Buy the domain separately, in your own name, and turn on auto renew.

Connecting the domain. This is the one. Your domain lives at one company and your site lives at another, and you have to introduce them with two DNS records. The change takes five minutes and then you wait, and waiting is where people panic, change the records again, and restart the clock. It is the single most common reason a finished site never goes live.

The honest math

Call it a weekend of your time, fifteen dollars for the domain, and somewhere between nothing and thirty dollars a month for hosting, depending on the route you take. If your weekend is worth more than a couple hundred dollars to you, the math stops favoring do it yourself pretty quickly.

But if you have the time and you like the idea of owning the thing end to end, build it. You will understand your own site better than most business owners ever do, and that is worth something on its own.

Common questions

Can I build my own small business website?

Yes, if you have a free weekend, a simple business to explain, and the willingness to maintain it. The work is nine steps, and none of them are hard on their own.

What is the hardest part of building your own website?

Connecting the domain. Your domain lives at one company and your site at another, and joining them means adding two DNS records and then waiting. Most abandoned projects stall right there.

Where should I buy a domain for my business?

Buy it at a registrar such as Namecheap, Porkbun, or Cloudflare rather than inside a website builder, keep it in your own name, and turn on auto renew.
