Why one page is not enough when people search for you

A single page trying to rank for six different searches usually ranks for none of them. That is the short version. Here is why, and what to do instead.

The problem with one page for everything

Say you are a roofer who does repairs, full replacements, storm damage, and gutters. If all four live on one page, that page is telling a search engine four different things at once, and competing with itself for each. Someone searching for storm damage repair gets a page that mentions it in one paragraph, next to three other services.

Meanwhile the competitor with a page about nothing but storm damage looks like the specialist, because on that page, they are.

One page per thing people actually search

The fix is boring and it works. Group the searches people really type into clusters, and give each cluster its own page with its own heading, its own proof, and its own contact path.

That means starting from what people search, not from your internal service list. Customers do not search for "comprehensive exterior solutions." They search for "roof leak repair Reno" at ten at night with a bucket under the drip.

What a good page needs beyond words

  • A title that names the service and the city, not "Home"
  • Proof a stranger can believe: reviews, photos of real work, licenses
  • A contact path that works on a phone, since that is where most people are
  • Speed, because a slow page loses people before they read a word
  • Your name, address, and phone matching your Google listing exactly

The part most people skip

A site is not finished at launch. Rankings build over months, and they build faster when the site keeps growing. That is what a monthly plan should be for: adding a page at a time, updating what is stale, and watching what people search for once you have real data.

If a monthly plan is not adding anything, it is just hosting with a nicer name. Ask what it includes before you pay for it.

How long it takes

Honest answer: months, not weeks, for organic search. What moves faster is everything else the site does, like looking credible to someone who already found you and is deciding whether to call. That part works the day it goes live.

Common questions

How many pages should a small business website have?

One page per thing people search for. A roofer searching customers use different words for repair, replacement, and storm damage, so each deserves its own page instead of one page competing with itself.

Why is my website not showing up on Google?

Usually three reasons: the page targets too many things at once, the title does not name the service and the city, or the site is new and has not earned any signals yet.

What should a website maintenance plan include?

Real additions, not just hosting. New pages over time, updates to what is stale, and attention to what people are actually searching once you have data.
