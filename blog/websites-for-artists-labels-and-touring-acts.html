Websites for artists, labels, and touring acts

Most web designers have never sent an EPK to a talent buyer and watched it get ignored. We have. That is the whole reason this is its own track.

An artist site has a different job

A business site converts a stranger into a customer. An artist site has to serve at least four audiences at once, and they want completely different things.

  • Fans want music, dates, and merch, fast, on a phone
  • Talent buyers and promoters want proof you can draw and a way to contact you in one click
  • Press wants a bio they can quote and photos they can actually print
  • Brands and sync want to know who you are and whether you are easy to work with

Build only for fans and you lose bookings. Build only for buyers and it feels like a resume.

Why most EPKs get ignored

They are a PDF that takes forty seconds to load, a Dropbox folder that asks for a login, or a page with a bio and nothing that proves anybody shows up. A buyer is deciding in under a minute, usually on a phone, usually between other things.

What gets read: one clear photo, a short bio with a real hook, two tracks that play immediately, recent dates with rooms and turnouts, and contact on the same screen. No downloads, no logins, no scavenger hunt.

Tour dates are a maintenance problem

Every artist site starts with current dates and ends up a year stale, because updating them is annoying. If the person maintaining it is you at two in the morning after a show, it has to be easy enough to do from a phone in five minutes, or it will not happen. That constraint shapes how we build it.

Two ways in

A one page EPK for artists who need something professional to send out now: bio, photos, music, dates, socials, booking contact. Fast to launch, easy to keep current.

A full artist site for acts with a catalog, a touring calendar, merch, and press to manage, where each of those deserves its own space.

Why we take this seriously

Before Hearty Kreation there was a label, tours, and years of production work. We have been on the other side of these emails, so when we say a buyer will not open that PDF, it is not a guess.
